Week one is strawberries and lettuce. Week two is radishes. A lot of radishes. They are the first root we can pull in the Driftless spring, they come up in 25 days, and for a few weeks every June they are most of what the hill has to give. So last Thursday I put a stack of index cards on the pickup table and asked you for your best radish trick. Here are the five that came back most.

1. Butter, salt, bread

The French way, and the way most of you wrote in. Slice the radishes thin, lay them on bread spread thick with good salted butter, and add flaky salt. That is it. The butter calms the bite. Eat it standing at the counter.

2. Roasted

A roasted radish tastes nothing like a raw one. Halve them, toss with oil and salt, and roast at 425 for 20 minutes until the cut sides brown. They go soft and sweet, like a small turnip. Toss the greens into the hot pan for the last minute.

3. Quick pickle

Slice them into a jar. Heat a cup of vinegar, a cup of water, a spoonful of sugar and a spoonful of salt until it dissolves, and pour it over. They are ready in an hour and keep a month, and they turn the whole jar pink. Priya from the La Crosse pickup adds a garlic scape.

4. Radish top pesto

The leaves are the best part, and most people throw them out. Blend two big handfuls with garlic, a handful of toasted sunflower seeds, hard cheese, lemon and olive oil. It is peppery and green and it goes on everything, eggs included.

5. In the salad, grated

Grated on the big holes of a box grater and tossed with the week's lettuce, a little honey and lemon, radish stops being a crunchy surprise and starts being a dressing.

Radishes are the farm saying hello. They are loud and a little rude and gone in a month, so enjoy them.

Next week brings snap peas and the first kale, and the radishes ease off until the fall crop in September. Keep the cards coming. The best ones go on the recipe card in your crate, with your name on it.
