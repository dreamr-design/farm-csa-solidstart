At 4:12 on the afternoon of September 8 the sky over the west ridge went the color of a bruise, and at 4:19 it was over. In between, hail the size of chickpeas came down the hollow sideways. Seven minutes. I timed it from the doorway of the machine shed, because there was nothing else to do.

When it stopped, Rosa and I walked the field in our boots. Here is what we found.

What we lost

Two beds of Carmen sweet peppers on the upper terrace took it straight on. Leaves stripped to the veins, fruit split open, stems snapped where the plants were heaviest. That is about 900 plants and maybe four weeks of peppers. The fall lettuce in the next bed is shredded too, but lettuce grows back. Peppers in September do not.

We also lost most of the sunflowers along the lane, which hurt more than it should have. They were just for looking at.

What we saved

The tomatoes. The storm came in on a line, and the field tomatoes sit one fence line east of the peppers. They got rain and wind and almost no ice. The high tunnels did their job: the plastic has a few dents and not one hole.

  • The squash field was untouched; the leaves were already dying back, so there was little to hit.
  • The roots were safe in the ground, as roots usually are.
  • The peppers on the lower terrace, half the crop, were sheltered by the windbreak we planted in 2018.

You plant a windbreak for a storm you will see once in eight years. This was the year.

Why your crate barely noticed

This is what a CSA is for. We plant 140 varieties so that no single storm can empty a crate. The week after the hail, Full shares got two fewer peppers and an extra pound of tomatoes from the tunnel. Half shares got the same peppers they always do, because Juniper packs the Half shares first.

We did not spray, and we will not. The damaged plants are cut back and composted, and the bed has been sown to winter rye, which will hold the soil and feed it until spring. In April those beds become onions.

Thank you to everyone who wrote that night. Members carry the weather with us, and that is the whole deal. If you want to share next year's weather, 2027 shares open February 1.

Tomás Rivera, co-owner, soil and machines
